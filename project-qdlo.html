¿Quién dio la orden? Digital Homage

About the project

Who Gave the Order? Digital Homage is a three-part transmedia work combining augmented reality, participatory performance and digital animation.

The project addresses the extrajudicial executions known as “Falsos Positivos” in Colombia. Through muralism, puppetry and mobile technologies, it transforms public spaces into places for memory, truth and collective reflection.

Historical context

The “Falsos Positivos” were people illegitimately killed and presented as combat casualties by members of Colombia’s armed forces. According to the Special Jurisdiction for Peace, at least 6,402 people were victims of this practice between 2002 and 2008.

The project accompanies the struggle of MAFAPO and the victims’ families to preserve memory, establish the truth and demand justice.

A work in three parts

01

The Mural

The iconic mural Who Gave the Order? is recreated through augmented reality in the viewer’s physical surroundings.

As visitors move along the mural, digital layers reveal names, figures and archival material. Two virtual puppet journalists guide the experience, explaining the context of the crimes and the continuing search for those responsible.

 02

The Magnitude of the Crime

The Magnitude of the Crime is a participatory performance exploring how systematic violence initially attracts public attention but gradually becomes normalized and forgotten.

Participants scroll through a digital memorial containing the victims’ names while a counter rises from 0 to 6,402. The familiar gesture of scrolling through a social-media feed becomes an act of remembrance.

How long does it take to reach the end of the list? How much time are we willing to give to the memory of each person?

 03

The Mothers’ Cry in the Streets

The Mothers’ Cry in the Streets is a multilingual digital projection inspired by MAFAPO and the mothers and relatives who continue to demand truth and justice.

The projection places their message directly in public space. AI-assisted animation and multilingual voices allow the work to travel while preserving its connection to Colombia.

Artistic approach

The project brings together two artistic traditions associated with social and political expression: muralism and puppetry.

Its digital adaptation preserves the imperfections of the painted wall and the gestures of the puppeteer. Rather than creating a polished digital reproduction, the project maintains the material character, resistance and emotional force of both practices.

Characters

Inocencio Calle

Coming from the beaches of Piangüita, Inocencio is a street reporter committed to listening to ordinary people and making social problems visible.

He sees himself as a journalist on the side of the people, although his innocence sometimes leads him to misunderstand situations and create controversy in his reports.

Cerdolfo Democracio

Democracio is a detective reporter and a faithful defender of law and order. After leaving the armed forces, he devoted himself to journalism, but his strong military training still leads him to approach every report as a mission.

He investigates violations of rules and accepted conduct in the streets. His strong temperament turns each report into an action-filled police operation.
